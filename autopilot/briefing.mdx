The briefing sits at the top of the page you land on when you open Whappy. It reports the week just gone: how many conversations happened, how many leads answered everything, and what needs a decision from you.

## Why it is not a dashboard

A dashboard answers questions you already thought to ask. The briefing exists to raise the things you would not have gone looking for.

That is also why it is short. Everything else Whappy does observes, decides or acts — and none of it says anything unless you go looking, which is what makes an automated system feel like a black box rather than a colleague.

## What it covers

**Your week in numbers** — conversations held, leads who answered everything, and the same broken down per campaign with a capture rate for each.

**What Autopilot did without being asked** — how many actions it took, how many were denied for lack of permission, how many Coach proposals are waiting for you, how many experiments are running, and how many changes were kept versus reverted.

**What needs attention** — the part worth reading first.

## Three levels of attention

Each item is one of three severities, and every item says what to do about it. An item that cannot say what to do is a worry rather than a briefing, and would leave you worse off than saying nothing.

| Severity | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Blocking** | Something is broken right now and you are losing leads until it is fixed. |
| **Waiting** | Something needs a decision, but nothing is broken meanwhile. |
| **For information** | Worth knowing, needs nothing from you. |

## Quiet weeks

When nothing happened and nothing needs doing, the briefing says so plainly rather than padding the space.

<Note>
    The briefing is generated weekly and shown in the dashboard. It is not yet emailed or sent over WhatsApp — that delivery channel is still being decided.
</Note>
